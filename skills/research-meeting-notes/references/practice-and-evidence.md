# Practice and evidence

| Source and locator | What the source supports | Application in these notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clark and colleagues, *A Transcription and Translation Protocol for Sensitive Cross-Cultural Team Research* (2017), “Adjusting Level of Transcription for Analysis” and Appendix A. [Article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5642906/) | The research team checked transcripts against audio, used conventions for overlapping or unintelligible speech, and retained hesitation when it conveyed meaning. | Identify the source you could actually verify. Choose the treatment of fillers to fit the question, preserve meaningful hesitations, and mark unresolved words. |
| Cornell University Learning Strategies Center, *The Cornell Note-taking System*, one-page handout. [Handout](https://lsc.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Cornell-NoteTaking-System.pdf) | The method separates the captured notes, cue questions, and a brief synthesis prepared afterward. | During office hours, record details and questions as they occur. Afterward, answer each guiding question in a short summary. The section names and layout can vary. |
| Purver and colleagues, *Detecting and Summarizing Action Items in Multi-Party Dialogue* (2007), §3, Table 1. [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2007.sigdial-1.4.pdf) | Their annotations distinguish task descriptions, ownership, timeframes and agreement across turns in naturally occurring research meetings. | Read the whole exchange before assigning a task. Distinguish a request from acceptance and record the speaker’s actual timing. |
| Zhong and colleagues, *QMSum: A New Benchmark for Query-based Multi-domain Meeting Summarization* (2021), §3. [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2021.naacl-main.472/) | Annotators associated specific meeting questions with relevant stretches of transcript and answers. The benchmark includes academic discussions. | Locate the passages bearing on each project or question before summarizing. Stable turn references are this skill’s way of making that association usable to readers. |
| Oral History Association, *OHA Statement on Ethics*, “Collaboration” and “Stewardship.” [Statement](https://oralhistory.org/oha-statement-on-ethics/) | The association describes review, agreed forms of access, redaction and authorization for public release of oral histories. | For oral-history projects, follow the applicable release agreement. For other settings, check the intended audience and any stated restrictions, and choose suitable public and restricted editions where needed. |

These sources address different tasks. Cornell describes a study method. QMSum defines a summarization benchmark. Neither evaluates this complete office-hours workflow.
